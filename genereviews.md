# Overall structure of a GeneReviews chapter

From top to bottom, the page is organized roughly like this:

1. **Title & synonyms**
   - Disease name and common alternative names.
   - Authors, last update date, estimated reading time.

2. **Summary**
   - Short, high‑yield overview of:
     - Clinical characteristics  
     - Diagnosis/testing  
     - Management  
     - Surveillance  
     - Genetic counseling  
   - This is essentially the “abstract.”

3. **Diagnosis**
   - **Suggestive findings** (clinical, imaging, histopathology, family history).
   - **Establishing the diagnosis**:
     - How to confirm the diagnosis.
     - **Molecular genetic testing strategy** (single-gene / multigene panel / exome).
     - Table 1: test methods and detection rates.

4. **Clinical Characteristics**
   - Detailed description of phenotype:
     - Neuropathy, cardiac, CNS, ocular, renal, others.
   - Tables summarizing frequency of features.
   - Sections on:
     - Onset and natural history  
     - Genotype–phenotype correlations  
     - Penetrance  
     - Nomenclature (old names vs current)  
     - Prevalence  

5. **Genetically Related (Allelic) Disorders**
   - Other conditions caused by **TTR variants** but not ATTRv (e.g. familial euthyroid hyperthyroxinemia).
   - Description of **wild-type ATTR** and how it differs.

6. **Differential Diagnosis**
   - **Other genes** and conditions that can look similar.
   - **Table 3a**: genetic mimics (other amyloidoses, CMT, Fabry, mitochondrial disease, HCM genes).
   - **Table 3b**: acquired mimics (AL amyloidosis, CIDP, POEMS, diabetic neuropathy, etc.).

7. **Management**
   - Evaluations after diagnosis (baseline work-up).
   - **Targeted therapies** (gene silencers, TTR stabilizers, transplant).
   - Supportive care for each organ system.
   - Surveillance schedule (what to check and how often).
   - Agents/circumstances to avoid.
   - Evaluation of relatives at risk.
   - Ongoing / experimental therapies (clinical trials).

8. **Genetic Counseling**
   - Mode of inheritance (autosomal dominant).
   - Recurrence risks for parents, sibs, offspring.
   - Predictive testing, testing in minors.
   - Prenatal and preimplantation genetic testing.
   - General counseling principles.

9. **Resources**
   - Patient organizations, foundations, registries.

10. **Molecular Genetics**
    - Gene and protein basics (TTR locus, protein function).
    - **Molecular pathogenesis** (tetramer destabilization, gain-of-function).
    - Key variants table (amino acid change, phenotype).
    - Links to databases (HGMD, ClinVar, locus-specific DB).

11. **Chapter Notes & References**
    - Author history, revision history.
    - Published guidelines and full reference list.

12. **Navigation/sidebar**
    - Links to section anchors (Summary, Diagnosis, Management, etc.).
    - Tools: PDF, citation export, glossary, search.

## What to focus on as a clinical laboratory geneticist

### A. Diagnosis & Testing Strategy (core for you)

**Relevant parts:**

- “Diagnosis/testing” in the **Summary**
- The full **Diagnosis** section:
  - Suggestive Findings  
  - Establishing the Diagnosis  
  - Molecular testing approaches (Option 1, Option 2)  
  - **Table 1: Molecular Genetic Testing Used in Hereditary Transthyretin Amyloidosis**

**Why it’s important:**

- Defines **what counts as diagnostic**:
  - Heterozygous *pathogenic / likely pathogenic* TTR variant + compatible findings.
- Shows which **test methods** are appropriate:
  - TTR sequencing as main test.
  - Large deletions/duplications not reported → del/dup has very low expected yield.
- Explicit link to **ACMG/AMP variant terminology**:
  - “Pathogenic” and “likely pathogenic” both used for clinical decision-making.

**How to read it:**

- Focus on:
  - Detection rates for each method (sequence vs del/dup).
  - The note that ATTRv is **gain-of-function** → variant types that make biological sense.
  - When to use:
    - Single-gene TTR sequencing  
    - A multigene panel  
    - Exome/genome when phenotype is atypical  

**Use in practice:**

- Designing test menus and reflex strategies.
- Justifying why your lab offers TTR sequencing (and what it *doesn’t* detect).
- Writing interpretation sections about **test limitations** and residual risk.

## Clinical Characteristics (for variant interpretation in context)

**Relevant parts:**

- Clinical characteristics in the **Summary**.
- The full **Clinical Characteristics** section:
  - Especially the early overview + Table 2 (frequency of features).
- **Genotype–phenotype correlations**
- **Penetrance**
- **Prevalence** and endemic vs non-endemic differences.

**Why it’s important:**

- Helps you decide if a **variant fits the phenotype**:
  - If a TTR variant is found in someone with purely diabetic neuropathy and no cardiomyopathy, you can question causality.
- Penetrance data show:
  - Not all heterozygotes manifest disease.
  - Penetrance differs markedly by region (e.g. Portugal vs Sweden).

**How to read it:**

- Note:
  - Typical age at onset in endemic vs non-endemic areas.
  - Phenotype clusters by variant (cardiac-dominant, leptomeningeal-dominant, etc.).
  - That penetrance is **age-dependent and population-dependent**.

**Use in practice:**

- Variant interpretation comments:
  - “This variant has been associated with predominantly cardiac amyloidosis…”
- Assessing whether a VUS in TTR could plausibly explain the observed phenotype.

## Genetically Related & Differential Diagnosis (for panel design and VUS context)

**Relevant parts:**

- **Genetically Related (Allelic) Disorders**:
  - TTR variants causing benign familial euthyroid hyperthyroxinemia.
- **Differential Diagnosis**:
  - **Table 3a**: genes that can mimic ATTRv (other amyloid genes, CMT, Fabry, mitochondrial diseases, HCM genes).
  - **Table 3b**: acquired conditions (AL amyloidosis, wild-type ATTR, CIDP, POEMS, diabetic neuropathy).

**Why it’s important:**

- Directly useful when building or updating:
  - **Amyloidosis panels**
  - **Hereditary neuropathy panels**
  - **Cardiomyopathy panels**
- Avoids misclassification:
  - Some TTR variants only alter thyroxine binding (lab finding) and are **clinically benign**.
- Prevents over‑attribution of symptoms to TTR when another disease is more likely.

**Use in practice:**

- Selecting genes for panels.
- Providing context when a TTR variant is found, but phenotype overlaps more with AL amyloidosis, Fabry, etc.

## Molecular Genetics (mechanism-level insight for lab work)

**Relevant parts:**

- **Table A** – TTR locus, protein, databases.
- **Molecular Pathogenesis** section.
- **Table 8** – key pathogenic TTR variants, phenotypes.

**Why it’s important:**

- Explains **mechanism of disease**:
  - Tetramer destabilization → monomer → amyloid fibrils.
  - **Gain-of-function** mechanism → typical pathogenic variants are missense; truncating variants usually **don’t** fit the mechanism.
- Clarifies:
  - Why stability and misfolding of TTR are central.
  - Why some variants are very destabilizing but not secreted well (impact on phenotype).
- Supports ACMG reasoning:
  - Functional data, mechanism-consistent variant type, etc.

**Use in practice:**

- Justifying classification decisions in reports:
  - “This missense variant lies in a functionally critical region of the TTR tetramer interface…”
- Designing internal documents or teaching material explaining ATTRv pathogenesis.

## Reading strategy for a lab geneticist

When you open any GeneReviews chapter, a practical reading order is:

1. **Summary**
   - Get the “big picture”: phenotype, test type, inheritance, treatment.

2. **Diagnosis**
   - Understand **Suggestive Findings**.
   - Learn the **testing algorithm** (single gene vs panel vs exome) and detection rates.

3. **Clinical Characteristics**
   - Focus on:
     - Main organ systems involved.
     - Genotype–phenotype correlations.
     - Penetrance and prevalence (especially for your region).

4. **Molecular Genetics**
   - Mechanism of disease.
   - Key variants and their associated phenotypes.

5. **Genetic Counseling**
   - Inheritance pattern and risk figures.
   - Testing recommendations for relatives.

6. **Differential Diagnosis / Genetically Related Disorders**
   - Use as needed for:
     - Panel design.
     - VUS interpretation and alternative diagnoses.
